Freezer Repairs

If a freezer is warming up, act promptly to protect the food inside. The checks below are limited to settings, door closure and visible ventilation; refrigeration-system repairs need a trained engineer.

Simple checks to try first

Only carry out checks that are user-accessible and described in your appliance manual. Do not dismantle the appliance.

The freezer is not cold enough

Check the temperature setting, make sure the door is fully shut and avoid adding large amounts of unfrozen food at once.

There is heavy frost

Check whether the door seal is clean and whether drawers or food are preventing the door from closing.

The freezer runs constantly

Check the door seal, room ventilation and whether the appliance has recently been loaded with warm food.

There is water around it

Look for an obvious source such as a defrost tray or nearby water spill, without removing panels.

Common freezer problems

These are possible causes, not a confirmed diagnosis. Several different faults can produce similar symptoms.

Not freezing

A thermostat, sensor, fan, defrost or sealed refrigeration-system fault may be involved.

Excessive frost

A damaged seal, door left ajar or defrost-system problem can cause frost build-up.

Temperature fluctuates

A sensor, fan, control or door-seal problem may affect temperature stability.

Unusual noise

An evaporator fan, compressor or other component may be faulty.

Leaking water

A drain, defrost-tray or water-related issue may be responsible.

Important safety advice

Keep the freezer door closed as much as possible if it is warming up, and move food to safe alternative storage where necessary. Stop using it if there is smoke, a burning smell, damaged wiring or repeated electrical tripping. Never open or pierce refrigeration pipes.

Never remove covers, handle exposed wiring or attempt repairs on live electrical equipment. For gas appliances, use a Gas Safe registered engineer.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if my freezer has stopped freezing?

Keep the door closed, protect the food by moving it to safe alternative storage if needed and arrange a prompt diagnosis.

Why is there ice building up inside?

A door seal that is not closing properly, frequent door opening or a defrost-system issue may cause frost.

Can I chip away thick ice?

Avoid using knives or sharp tools because they can damage the appliance and refrigeration system. Follow the manufacturer’s defrost instructions.
